# 02: Prior art. Who has trained end-to-end ASR to output clean / written-form text directly?

## Answer

Yes, it has been done, but mainly for disfluency removal on conversational speech, and only in research papers plus one hobby-scale open model. Nobody has published a dictation model that does all of Proofed's tasks in one pass. Jamshid Lou and Johnson (EMNLP Findings, Nov 2020) trained CTC, LSTM seq2seq and Transformer ASR on fluent Switchboard targets. The models learned to output fluent text, but they were *slightly worse* than ASR followed by a disfluency detector, which counts as a negative result [S0201, S0202]. Bassi et al. (arXiv, Sep 2023) reversed that using a pretrained HuBERT-CTC model: the end-to-end model scored 12.2% WER against 13.1% for the two-stage model [S0203]. Pretrained acoustic models are therefore the reason the end-to-end route now works. The project closest to Proofed is **FluentWhisper** (HF Space, June 2026), a rank-16 LoRA on whisper-large-v3-turbo trained on about 23k synthetic Kokoro-TTS pairs. Its README reports 3.4% against 9.4% WER on DisfluencySpeech, and it warns that the adapter deletes aggressively and loses repeated digits and some self-repairs [S0205]. For numbers and written form, the main end-to-end evidence is mixed. Google (Peyser et al., 2019) found written-domain RNN-T was far worse on long numerics than spoken-domain RNN-T plus a small neural denormer (50.9 vs 11.3 WER on TAIL_LONG), but slightly better on real-audio numeric sets [S0206]. Cui et al. (EUSIPCO 2025) showed one conditioned decoder can emit both punctuated and normalised forms [S0207]. Explicit dictation self-corrections ("Tuesday, no, Wednesday") and asides have **no end-to-end ASR paper I could find**. Every shipped product that documents its architecture uses ASR followed by an LLM or text model: Superwhisper S1, Wispr Flow and AssemblyAI [S0215, S0217, S0218, S0219].

## Evidence

### (a) Disfluency removal inside end-to-end ASR
- Jamshid Lou & Johnson, "End-to-End Speech Recognition and Disfluency Removal", Findings of EMNLP 2020 (Nov 2020), pp. 2051–2061. They trained ASR to map disfluent speech straight to fluent transcripts on Switchboard (260 h, of which 1,126 conversations have gold disfluency labels) plus Fisher with silver labels [S0201, S0202]. Confirmed.
- Negative result. On the SWBD test set, the Transformer end-to-end model scored FER 11.6 and DER 24.0. The pipeline (ASR plus disfluency detector) scored FER 12.1 and DER 22.2, and DER is the disfluency-removal metric. Read on DER, the end-to-end model removes disfluencies worse than the pipeline across CTC, seq2seq and Transformer. The authors call the gap "slightly worse" [S0201]. Confirmed: table read from the ar5iv HTML. Column alignment was checked only through the fetch summary.
- Bassi et al., arXiv 2309.04516 (8 Sep 2023), preprint with no venue. HuBERT with a CTC head, fine-tuned on fluent Switchboard targets: end-to-end 12.2% WER / 7.3% CER against two-stage 13.1% / 7.6%. They report that the choice of pretraining objective strongly affects how well the model adapts. They did not report FER/DER, citing time constraints [S0203]. Confirmed.
- Qian et al., "End-to-End Spoken Grammatical Error Correction", arXiv 2506.18532 (Jun 2025; accepted in *Speech Communication*). They compare cascaded, partial-cascaded and end-to-end Whisper systems for producing fluent and grammar-corrected text, and grow the data by pseudo-labelling from 77 h to over 2,500 h [S0204]. Confirmed at abstract level only. The fluent-target Whisper numbers were not read.
- FluentWhisper (build-small-hackathon Space, created 2026-06-13; adapter `pradachan/whisper-large-v3-turbo-disfluency-lora`). Training data: LibriSpeech text with injected disfluencies (custom scripts plus the LARD tool), voiced by Kokoro TTS across 54 voices, about 23k pairs, plus about 4.5k DisfluencySpeech train rows. Results: 3.4% vs 9.4% WER on the DisfluencySpeech test set (N=250, single acted speaker). The README states that vanilla Whisper already deletes most um/uh, and that the adapter loses intentional repeats, digits and some self-repairs [S0205]. Confirmed from the Space README fetched with curl, since the Space itself is paused. These numbers are self-reported and not peer reviewed.
- DisfluencySpeech (arXiv 2406.08820, Jun 2024) is a single speaker re-performing about 10 h of Switchboard material. It ships three transcripts with progressively more removed: non-speech events, then non-sentence elements, then false starts. It is a ready-made benchmark with clean targets [S0226]. Confirmed.
- Whisper already strips filled pauses implicitly, which is a property of its weakly supervised training transcripts. A 2023 evaluation found "Whisper removes the ums and uhs" but keeps most other disfluencies [S0224]. Kid-Whisper says the same and attributes it to Radford et al. [S0225]. Confirmed in secondary sources. I did not read the Whisper paper's own wording.

### (b) Inverse text normalisation / written form learned end-to-end
- Peyser, Zhang, Sainath, Wu (Google), "Improving Performance of End-to-End ASR on Numeric Sequences", Interspeech 2019. Written-domain RNN-T had trouble with rare and long numerics: the baseline W0 scored 92.7 WER on TAIL_LONG. Spoken-domain training plus a small neural denormer scored best on long synthetic sets (11.3 vs 50.9 for the best written model). The written model was slightly better on real-audio sets (NUMERICS 6.3 vs 7.2) [S0206]. Confirmed, with the caveat that the extracted table columns were somewhat misaligned. Reading for Proofed: digits emitted directly is the hard path for long or rare numbers, and TTS-augmented numeric data is the fix Google used.
- Cui, Sheikh, Sadeghi, Vincent, arXiv 2311.17741 (Nov 2023; EUSIPCO 2025). A single decoder, conditioned on output type, emits punctuated/cased or normalised transcripts: 42% relative PC-WER reduction against Whisper-base, and it still works with 5% punctuated data at +2.42 absolute PC-WER [S0207]. Confirmed. This is style-conditioned end-to-end output, but it does not do number ITN.
- Separate post-ASR text models remain the industry norm. Microsoft's Four-in-One (arXiv 2210.15063, Oct 2022) is a transformer tagger for ITN, punctuation, capitalisation and disfluency, followed by WFST grammars [S0208]. Universal-2-TF (arXiv 2501.05948, Jan 2025) is a two-stage neural text formatter for commercial ASR [S0209]. Confirmed as text-side and not end-to-end. The AssemblyAI affiliation of Universal-2-TF is inferred from the name and was not stated on the page.

### (c) Self-correction / repair handling
- In the Switchboard work, "repairs" (the reparandum and interregnum such as "uh I mean" are deleted) are part of the fluent target, so [S0201]–[S0203] already cover conversational self-repair end-to-end. Confirmed for the Switchboard annotation scheme as described in [S0201].
- No paper found that trains end-to-end ASR on *dictation-style explicit corrections* ("Haifa, I mean Tiberias", "scratch that"). The searches tried were "self-repair correction speech recognition end-to-end … dictation" and "speech repair dictation dataset LLM 2025 2026 scratch that". The nearest dataset is SPACER (arXiv 2503.16745, 2025), which covers Switchboard single-word substitution repairs. This is an empty search: treat it as weak evidence of absence.
- Text-side models handle it, and they show the failure mode. S1-mini "resolves self-corrections" ("the meeting is on Tuesday I mean Thursday" becomes "...Thursday") [S0215, S0216]. The simplewords-dictation-cleanup v1 model (Qwen QLoRA, after ASR) showed "self-correction inversion", keeping the replaced side, and v2 was retrained with explicit self-correction examples [S0223]. Confirmed. This is a concrete negative result Proofed should test for.
- FluentWhisper reports losing "some self-repairs" [S0205].

### (d) Removing non-directed / off-talk speech
- Device-directed speech detection is a separate classifier in all the work found. Amazon: Mallidi et al., Interspeech 2018, uses acoustic, 1-best and decoder features and reaches 5.2% EER [S0211]. Apple: Wagner et al., arXiv 2312.03632 (Dec 2023), feeds ASR 1-best, decoder signals and an audio encoder into a frozen LLM with LoRA, using 80k or fewer examples [S0212]. SAS (arXiv 2604.08412, Apr 2026) is an on-device gate before ASR: audio-only F1 0.86, on proprietary data [S0214]. Confirmed. None removes asides inside the transcript.
- Closest to "inside the ASR": Google, Arumugam et al., ASRU 2023 (arXiv 2312.11123). They add primary and non-primary speaker tokens to the RNN-T vocabulary. For the Dictation domain, "the ASR model is expected to not transcribe the non-primary speakers". Dictation WER stayed at 3.6 and long-form deletions fell by 55% [S0210]. Confirmed. Note that this works on *other speakers*, not the *same speaker* turning aside, which is Proofed's case.
- Same-speaker addressee shift is detectable from prosody. Shriberg et al. (Interspeech 2012) found "users change speaking style as they shift addressees" and reported prosodic EER 12.6% against lexical 23.1% on ASR output [S0213]. Confirmed. Inferred: an acoustic model like Parakeet has the prosodic cue available, which supports the experimental tier, but nobody has shown it working inside ASR output.

### (e) Products / models marketing clean dictation
- **Superwhisper S1** (blog 2026-08-19): two stages. S1-Voice is the ASR, cloud-hosted and trained in-house. S1-Language (cloud) or S1-mini (local) does the cleanup [S0215]. S1-mini is a Qwen3-0.6B fine-tune, takes text in and gives text out, Apache 2.0, with 94.8% token accuracy on 7,519 held-out cases [S0216]. Confirmed as ASR plus text model.
- **Wispr Flow**: two stages. The CTO post (2025-09-26) budgets "E2E ASR inference" under 200 ms, "E2E LLM inference" under 200 ms, and networking under 200 ms, within a 700 ms total [S0217]. Baseten's case study says the cleanup step is a fine-tuned Llama [S0218]. Confirmed as ASR plus LLM, in the cloud.
- **AssemblyAI** (blog 2026-09-02): filler removal is on by default in the ASR output (`disfluencies: true` keeps fillers). Self-corrections are handled by a separate LLM rewrite pass [S0219]. Confirmed. Inferred: filler removal by default may be inside the Universal model, but the page does not say where.
- **Google AI Edge Eloquent** (iOS, reported 2026-04-07): removes fillers and "corrects mid-sentence errors" offline, with an optional Gemini cloud cleanup [S0220]. The article does not name the models or say whether one or two run on device. Unknown. Other coverage claims "Gemma-based ASR", which I did not confirm.
- **Apple** systemwide dictation (WWDC, 2026-06-08) runs on a "new Apple Intelligence model created based on Google's Gemini" and corrects spelling, punctuation and capitalisation. The article does not say whether it removes fillers or handles self-corrections, or whether it is one model [S0221]. Unknown.
- **Aqua Voice Avalon**: Aqua's own ASR model. The guide says nothing about cleanup or architecture [S0222]. Unknown.
- **Willow**: removes fillers and fixes grammar, with an on-device offline mode. No model or architecture is stated [S0227]. Unknown.
- **Google Recorder**: on-device Gemini Nano is documented for summaries. I found no source for filler removal (search only, not cited).

## Disagreements and gaps

- 2020 and 2023 disagree. Jamshid Lou & Johnson found end-to-end worse than the pipeline, and Bassi et al. found it better. The setups differ: models trained from scratch versus pretrained HuBERT, and FER/DER versus WER on fluent references. The Bassi comparison omits disfluency-specific metrics. Proofed's starting point, a pretrained Parakeet, matches the setting where end-to-end won.
- Every end-to-end disfluency result is on conversational telephone speech (Switchboard) or one acted speaker (DisfluencySpeech). None is on dictation, where fillers are sparser and corrections are explicit.
- Gaps no source covered:
  - No published Parakeet/TDT/RNN-T fine-tune for clean output was found. FluentWhisper is attention-decoder Whisper.
  - No end-to-end work on dictation-style explicit corrections.
  - No end-to-end same-speaker aside removal.
  - No end-to-end paragraph output.
  - No study stacks several of these tasks in one fine-tune and measures interference.
- Product architecture is undisclosed for Apple, Google Eloquent, Aqua and Willow. "One model" is not claimed by any vendor source I read.
- The Peyser table was read through extracted HTML with misaligned columns, so the exact cell values carry some risk.

## Confidence

Medium. The academic core is confirmed from primary pages and agrees on the shape: end-to-end disfluency removal is feasible, was weaker than pipelines before pretraining, and is competitive after it. Written-form numerics are harder end-to-end without targeted data. Two weaknesses lower confidence. First, the closest analogue, FluentWhisper, is self-reported and hackathon-scale. Second, the absence findings for (c) and (d) rest on a handful of searches, and most product claims are vendor marketing with architectures undisclosed.
