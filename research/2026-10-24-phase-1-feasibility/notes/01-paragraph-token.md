# Paragraphs and the tokenizer: can Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2 emit paragraph breaks?

## Answer

No, not as it stands. I extracted the SentencePiece model from the published `parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2.nemo` and inspected it. It is a 1,024-piece BPE vocabulary (plus blank, 1,025 outputs, and 5 TDT duration outputs). It has no user-defined or control symbols and no byte fallback. No piece contains `\n`, `\r`, tab, U+2028/2029, `¶` or `§`. Its `nmt_nfkc` normaliser turns `\n` into a space before tokenising, so a newline in a training manifest is lost without any error [S0101][S0102].

Option (b) is the cheapest and needs no architecture change: a sentinel built from tokens that already exist. The best candidate is `^` (id 918). It is a single piece, round-trips exactly (`"Hello. ^ New"` comes back unchanged) and almost never occurs in speech transcripts. Train on targets like `… end of point. ^ Next point …` and swap ` ^ ` for `\n\n` after decoding; that swap is a one-line string replace, not a model [S0101]. `¡` (1012) and `¿` (904) also work as single tokens. `¶`, `§`, `|`, `#`, `~`, `@`, `*`, `_`, `<`, `>`, `[`, `{` and `→` all encode to `<unk>` and are unusable. A double space also survives (it encodes as `▁ ▁`, because `remove_extra_whitespaces` is off), but it is fragile: NeMo, Lhotse and downstream text cleaners routinely collapse whitespace.

Option (a), NeMo `change_vocabulary`, keeps only the encoder. In the current NeMo `main`, `EncDecRNNTBPEModel.change_vocabulary` deletes the joint and the prediction network and rebuilds both from config, so they are freshly initialised. Proofed would then have to relearn all decoding from its small dataset, which defeats the purpose. That code path also rebuilds the loss as `RNNTLoss(num_classes=…)` without `loss_name="tdt"` [S0103]. A middle route is to add one user-defined piece to the SentencePiece model and grow the trained joint output layer and decoder embedding by one row by hand ("weight surgery"). This keeps the trained weights, but the new row has to be inserted at index 1024, before the blank (which moves to 1025) and the 5 duration logits [S0103][S0104]. I found no NeMo built-in for this.

Option (c), a different base: Whisper's GPT-2 BPE does have a newline token (`Ċ`, id 198), and LLM-decoder ASR models (Canary-Qwen, Qwen3-ASR, Voxtral) inherit newline tokens from their LLM tokenizers. I found no open ASR model documented to emit paragraph breaks from its normal training [S0105][S0106]. Among commercial APIs, the paragraphs I could document are API-side segmentation of a finished, punctuated transcript, not model output: AssemblyAI's `/paragraphs` endpoint and Deepgram's `paragraphs` / `smart_format` [S0107][S0108][S0109]. Prompted audio LLMs such as Gemini can produce paragraphs because they are general LLMs; that is the large-model route Proofed exists to avoid.

## Evidence

- **Confirmed (local check):** the stock tokenizer is SentencePiece BPE (`model_type=2`), with `vocab_size=1024`, `byte_fallback=False`, no `user_defined_symbols` or `control_symbols`, normaliser `nmt_nfkc`, `add_dummy_prefix=True` and `remove_extra_whitespaces=False`. No piece contains `\n`, `\r`, `\t`, U+2028, U+2029, `¶` or `§` [S0101].
- **Confirmed:** `"Hello.\nNew paragraph."` encodes the same as a plain space and decodes as `"Hello. New paragraph."`. A tab is dropped the same way. `\n\n` becomes `▁ ▁` [S0101].
- **Confirmed:** the non-alphanumeric single pieces are `▁` 819, `,` 839, `.` 841, `'` 844, `?` 854, `-` 857, `:` 875, `!` 885, `%` 886, `$` 888, `/` 892, `£` 895, `¿` 904, `^` 918, `€` 919 and `¡` 1012. Ids 891–1023 are accented Latin, Greek and Cyrillic single characters [S0101].
- **Confirmed:** `^`, `¡` and `¿` round-trip exactly as standalone pieces (`▁` + `^`). `¶`, `§`, `|`, `#`, `~`, `@`, `*`, `_`, `[`, `{`, `<`, `>` and `→` all become `<unk>` [S0101].
- **Confirmed:** the csukuangfj sherpa-onnx export's `tokens.txt` (1,025 lines, last line `<blk> 1024`) matches the `.nemo` `tokenizer.vocab` piece for piece. The sherpa `tokens.txt` is therefore a valid proxy for this tokenizer [S0101][S0102].
- **Confirmed:** `model_config.yaml` gives target `EncDecRNNTBPEModel`, `loss_name: tdt`, durations [0,1,2,3,4], `joint.num_classes: 1024`, `num_extra_outputs: 5`, `decoder.vocab_size: 1024` and `blank_as_pad: true`, saved with `nemo_version: 2.4.0rc0` [S0101].
- **Confirmed (code):** `EncDecRNNTBPEModel.change_vocabulary` runs `del self.joint` and `del self.decoder`, then calls `from_config_dict(...)` with the new vocabulary size, which makes new randomly initialised modules. Its docstring says it "changes only decoder and leaves encoder and pre-processing modules unchanged" [S0103].
- **Confirmed (code), consequence inferred:** that function rebuilds `self.loss = RNNTLoss(num_classes=self.joint.num_classes_with_blank - 1)` with no `loss_name` or `loss_kwargs`. `__init__` and the char-model `change_vocabulary` both pass `loss_name="tdt"` and subtract `num_extra_outputs`. Untested: calling it on Parakeet TDT may silently switch to the default RNNT loss with the wrong class count, so the loss should be re-set by hand after the call [S0103].
- **Confirmed (code):** the RNNT joint's final layer is `Linear(joint_hidden, num_classes + 1 + num_extra_outputs)`, which is 640 → 1030 here. The decoder blank index is `vocab_size`, with `Embedding(vocab_size + 1, …, padding_idx=blank)`. A token added by surgery must go in before the blank and duration rows [S0104][S0101].
- **Confirmed:** the model card says output includes punctuation and capitalisation and lists training data as Granary, about 120k h. It does not mention newlines, paragraphs or vocabulary size [S0110].
- **Confirmed (local check):** Whisper large-v3's tokenizer has `Ċ` (newline) at id 198 and `¶` at id 114 [S0105].
- **Inferred, weak source:** a developer tutorial says raw Whisper output arrives with "no paragraph breaks", so having the token does not mean the model was trained to use it [S0106].
- **Confirmed:** AssemblyAI `GET /v2/transcript/{id}/paragraphs` takes an existing transcript and "will attempt to semantically segment your transcript into paragraphs". It sits beside a `/sentences` endpoint of the same kind, which makes it API-side segmentation [S0107].
- **Confirmed:** Deepgram `paragraphs=true` identifies paragraphs "based on punctuation" plus speaker and channel changes, and turns on punctuation. `smart_format` applies Paragraphs at minimum for English [S0108][S0109].

## Disagreements and gaps

- I did not train or load the model in NeMo. The surgery route and the `change_vocabulary` TDT-loss concern come from reading code on NeMo `main` (fetched 2026-10-24), not from a run. The repo's training setup may pin a different NeMo version (`audio-dictation/English-Hebrew-Codeswitching-Parakeet-Finetune`); check that version's file.
- Hebrish/Jachnun's sherpa `tokens.txt` is gated (HTTP 401 without login). I could not compare it, so its use of the stock tokenizer is assumed, not confirmed. I used the stock `.nemo` and the csukuangfj export instead.
- `^` is untested as a learned target. Unknowns: whether a 0.6B transducer learns to emit a pause- or topic-driven token from a few hundred examples, and whether stock Parakeet already emits `^` in some contexts (Granary pseudo-labels could contain it). A quick check: decode a test set and grep for `^`, `¡` and `¿`.
- I found no documentation either way on whether gpt-4o-transcribe, ElevenLabs Scribe, Voxtral, Canary-Qwen or Qwen3-ASR emit newlines natively. Searches for gpt-4o-transcribe and ElevenLabs paragraph behaviour came back empty, and ElevenLabs' API reference lists `additional_formats` without defining its options. These are unknowns, not negatives.
- Paragraph boundaries are partly a matter of meaning rather than sound. Whether an ASR decoder with no LLM can place them well is sub-question 04's territory. The repo's text-side `ASR-Paragraph-Breaks` (F1 0.78) is the baseline to beat.

## Confidence

High that the tokenizer lacks newline and paragraph tokens and that `^` is a usable single-token sentinel: checked directly on the published `.nemo` tokenizer and cross-checked against a second export. Medium on the `change_vocabulary` and surgery details: read from current source, never run. Low to medium on the native-paragraph survey: commercial behaviour comes from docs, and open models' newline behaviour comes from tokenizers and one weak source.
